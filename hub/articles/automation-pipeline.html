Step 0: Put the Site on Managed Hosting

The pipeline needs a home before anything else can go live. Step 1: Research — Find a Problem Worth Solving

Research is the step that determines whether everything downstream is worth doing. The job here is to find a specific problem that a reachable group of people has, that they are already spending money or significant time trying to solve, and that you can credibly address with a digital product. The raw material exists in public: reviews on marketplaces and app stores, questions in forums and community groups, complaints in comment sections, and the gap between what competitor products promise and what customers say they actually got.

AI is useful here for processing volume. Feed it a large batch of reviews or forum threads and ask it to cluster recurring complaints, extract the exact language customers use to describe their problem, and surface patterns you might miss reading manually. That language is valuable — it tells you how to write the sales page and the email sequence later. What AI cannot do is tell you whether the problem is worth solving as a business. That judgment — is there willingness to pay, is the audience reachable, is there a credible angle for us specifically — belongs to the human running the research.

No specific tool is linked at this step because the AI layer here can be handled by whatever general-purpose model you already use. The more important input is the research sources: the reviews, the forums, the competitor offer pages you read carefully before you write a single word of your own.

Step 3: Content — Articles and Guides That Compound

Content is the primary traffic engine for this kind of business. Articles and guides indexed in search accumulate readers over time in a way that paid ads do not. The job at this step is to publish pieces that answer the specific questions your research surfaced — written in the language real customers use, structured to match the intent of someone who is close to making a decision about a tool or approach.

AI drafts from structured briefs built on the Step 1 research. A brief specifies the audience, the problem being addressed, the angle, the claims that need to be supported and the sources to draw on. With a solid brief, AI can produce a usable working draft in a fraction of the time it would take to write from scratch. The human's job after that draft exists is to edit for accuracy, adjust for voice, cut anything that overstates, and add the judgment and direct observation that make a piece worth reading. This hub is itself an example of that format: every piece published here starts with a brief, goes through AI-assisted drafting, and then gets checked and edited by a person before it goes live.

One rule we apply consistently: every claim about a tool — pricing, features, plan names — gets verified against the vendor's current pricing and documentation pages before publishing. Tool details change, and publishing outdated information damages trust more than it costs to fix in the editing pass.

The best-performing content pieces have a second life as short avatar-driven explainer videos. Video reaches people who prefer watching to reading, and a clip embedded in an article or shared on a platform where the article itself would not appear extends the reach of a piece that has already proven it resonates. AI avatar tools make this practical for a small team without a studio, a camera setup or a presenter willing to appear on screen.

Step 5: Email — The Sequence That Sells While You Sleep

Leads from content and video enter an automated email sequence. The structure is not complicated: a welcome message that sets expectations, one or two value-focused emails that address the problem the subscriber came in with, an offer email, and a follow-up for people who did not act on the first. The sequence runs automatically once someone opts in, which means it is working at hours and in time zones you are not covering manually.

Step 6: The Sales Page

Each offer gets one sales page, built inside the funnel tool. The structure is not novel and does not need to be: problem, outcome the product delivers, proof that it works, price, guarantee. AI is useful for drafting variants of the headline, the opening problem statement and the offer description. The human decides what claims go on the page, what the price is, and what the guarantee covers — because those are business decisions with real consequences, not production tasks.

The rule at this step is the same as at the content step: every claim on the page has to be supportable. That means no income projections, no results you cannot back up, and no implied guarantees beyond what you have actually committed to. A shorter page with honest, specific claims outperforms a longer page with vague superlatives, and it creates fewer problems when customers arrive expecting what was described.

Step 7: Delivery and Follow-Up

When a payment clears, three things happen automatically: the product is delivered (Systeme.io handles course access and file delivery natively), a thank-you and onboarding email sequence starts, and a request for a review or referral is queued for several days after delivery — far enough out that the buyer has had time to actually use what they purchased. None of this requires a person to be present at the moment of sale.

Support questions that come in after delivery are not just support tickets — they are research. The questions buyers ask most often are a direct signal about where the product or the sales page created unclear expectations. Routing those questions back into Step 1 and using them to improve both the offer and the content that attracts the next buyer is how the pipeline improves over time rather than staying static.

Each step feeds the next, and the output of the last step — buyer feedback — feeds back into the first real work step. Running it once teaches you where the bottlenecks are in your specific niche. What Automation Does Not Do

Automation does not validate your niche. It does not guarantee that anyone will buy what you have built. It does not replace the editing judgment that separates a piece of content that earns trust from one that just exists. And it does not remove the need for someone to own the offer — to stand behind the claims on the sales page, to answer support questions honestly, and to decide when something is not working and needs to change.

The pipelines that work are the ones where a human checks the AI's output at every step, reads it critically, cuts what is wrong or vague, and makes the calls that require actual knowledge of the business and the customer. Automation makes production faster. The judgment about what to produce, what to claim and what to sell is yours.
