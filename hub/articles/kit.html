Kit Review: Email Automation for Creators Who Sell Digital Products

Kit — the platform formerly known as ConvertKit, rebranded in October 2024 — is an email marketing tool designed specifically for creators rather than general e-commerce businesses or enterprise marketing teams. Where most email platforms try to serve everyone, Kit has stayed deliberately narrow: it wants to be the tool that handles your list, your sequences, and your digital product sales in one place. That focus shapes everything from the interface to the pricing model.

In our automation pipeline, Kit fills the email role. After someone purchases a digital product, we need a system that can tag that buyer, deliver the product or access instructions, start a timed follow-up sequence, and eventually re-engage them with broadcast emails. Kit is built to do exactly that chain of events — and understanding where it fits (and where it stops) is the core of this review.

How It Fits an Automation Pipeline

The practical scenario we're describing is a solo creator selling a digital product — a guide, a template pack, a mini-course — through a checkout tool. The buyer pays, and something needs to happen automatically: they get a confirmation and delivery email, they're tagged as a buyer so they don't receive new-subscriber pitches for something they already own, and they enter a short follow-up sequence designed to help them use what they bought and, later, hear about related offers.

In Kit, that flow starts with a form or an external trigger. If you're selling through Kit Commerce (Kit's built-in product selling feature), a purchase automatically tags the buyer and can drop them into a visual automation. If you're using an external checkout tool, Kit connects through its native integrations or via webhook so the same tagging and sequencing logic fires on purchase.

The visual automation builder is where this comes together. You build a flowchart: someone enters when they receive a tag, they go through a waiting period, they receive email one of your welcome and delivery sequence, then email two a few days later, then they exit the sequence and get added to your general broadcast list. Each step is visible on a canvas, which makes it straightforward to audit and edit without digging through nested settings menus.

Sequences in Kit are sets of pre-written emails sent on a schedule relative to when the subscriber entered — day zero, day two, day seven, and so on. This is distinct from broadcasts, which go out to your whole list (or a tagged segment) at a specific point in time. For a post-purchase pipeline you use sequences for the timed onboarding and follow-up, and broadcasts for ongoing communication like new content or limited-time offers.

Tagging is central to keeping this clean. Kit's subscriber model is tag-based, not list-based, meaning one subscriber can hold multiple tags without being duplicated across separate lists. A buyer gets a "customer" tag. If they buy a second product, they get a second tag. Your sequences and automations use those tags to decide who gets what, so the same subscriber can be in your general newsletter and excluded from a sales sequence for something they already own — all without manual management.

For a solo creator running content, email, and digital product sales without a team, this architecture handles the repetitive parts of the follow-up cycle. You set the sequence once, and it runs for every new buyer without additional work at the moment of sale.

Key Features

Automations

Visual automation builder with a flowchart canvas. Triggers include form submissions, tag additions, purchases, and link clicks. Used to build post-purchase sequences, re-engagement flows, and segmentation logic.

Sequences

Evergreen email sequences tied to subscriber entry points rather than calendar dates. Each email in the sequence fires at a set delay from entry. Sequences are the backbone of an automated onboarding or follow-up pipeline.

Broadcasts

One-off emails sent to your full list or a filtered segment. Available on all plans including the free tier. Used for newsletters, announcements, and time-sensitive promotions.

Subscriber Tagging and Segmentation

Tag-based subscriber model. Subscribers exist once on your list and can carry multiple tags. Segments are built by filtering on tags, so the same subscriber can receive different content paths without duplication.

Landing Pages and Forms

Unlimited landing pages and opt-in forms included on all plans. Used to capture subscribers and feed them into automations.

Kit Commerce

Built-in product selling for digital downloads and paid newsletters. Handles checkout and purchase-triggered tagging directly within Kit. Carries a transaction fee (see Pricing).

Subscriber Scoring and Deliverability Reporting (Pro only)

The Pro plan adds engagement scoring for subscribers and more detailed deliverability reporting, alongside a newsletter referral system and access to the Kit sponsor network.

Pricing

Kit prices by subscriber count, not by the number of emails you send. All three plans are on the same subscriber base; moving up unlocks features, not just higher send volume.

Free (Newsletter Plan)

$0 for up to 10,000 subscribers. Includes unlimited landing pages, forms, and broadcasts. You can sell digital products through Kit Commerce. Does not include email sequences or visual automations.

Creator

From $39/month (billed monthly) or $33/month (billed yearly, $390/year) at 1,000 subscribers. Adds unlimited visual automations, email sequences, integrations, and removes Kit branding. At 10,000 subscribers, Creator costs $139/month monthly or $116/month billed yearly. Includes a 14-day free trial, no card required, and free migration from other tools.

Pro (formerly Creator Pro)

From $79/month (billed monthly) or $66/month (billed yearly, $790/year) at 1,000 subscribers. Adds a newsletter referral system, subscriber engagement scoring, deliverability reporting, and access to Kit's sponsor network. At 10,000 subscribers, Pro costs $189/month monthly or $158/month billed yearly. Same 14-day free trial and free migration included.

Commerce Transaction Fee

Selling through Kit Commerce carries a fee of 3.5% + 30 cents per sale on all plans.

Note: Kit raised its prices in 2025 — Creator at 1,000 subscribers previously started at $29/month, so older reviews will quote lower figures. Prices can change; always verify the current rates on Kit's pricing page before making a decision.

Pros

  • Built for the creator workflow. Tag-based segmentation, visual automations, and sequences are all oriented around the way solo creators actually manage content, products, and audiences — not around enterprise campaign management.
  • Sequences and automations are well integrated. The visual canvas makes it easy to see and edit a post-purchase flow without digging through separate menus for each trigger, sequence, and tag rule.
  • Generous free tier for simple use cases. Up to 10,000 subscribers at no cost, with unlimited landing pages, forms, and broadcasts, is a large entry point for creators who are still building an audience and not yet running automations.
  • No subscriber duplication. One subscriber, multiple tags. This keeps your list clean and your segmentation accurate as buyers accumulate multiple products or interest tags over time.
  • 14-day free trial, no card required, with free migration. Lower friction to try it against your existing setup, and you don't have to rebuild your list manually if you're switching platforms.
  • Digital product selling built in. Kit Commerce means buyers can purchase and get auto-tagged without needing a separate product delivery tool, though the transaction fee is a real cost to factor in.

Cons

  • The free plan can't run a post-purchase pipeline. The 10,000-subscriber free tier looks generous, but sequences and visual automations are paid-only features. If you're automating anything beyond a one-off broadcast after a purchase, you're on a paid plan from day one. The free tier is effectively for newsletters, not automation.
  • Per-subscriber pricing scales steeply. Creator at 1,000 subscribers starts at $39/month. At 10,000 subscribers on the same plan, that's $139/month — a 3.5× increase for a 10× growth in list size. If your list grows quickly, the cost climbs faster than it might on flat-fee platforms.
  • Prices increased in 2025. Kit raised Creator from $29 to $39/month at the 1,000-subscriber tier. This matters if you're budgeting against older estimates or reviews.
  • Email-first, not a full sales stack. Kit handles the email side well, but if you need a full funnel — sales pages with detailed customization, upsells, affiliate management, complex checkout flows — you'll still need external tools. Kit's built-in commerce and landing pages cover basic cases, not complex ones.
  • Commerce transaction fee adds up on volume. At 3.5% + 30¢ per sale, the fee is noticeable if you're processing many smaller transactions. At higher volume or higher price points you'll want to compare this against dedicated checkout platforms.

Verdict

Kit is a focused tool and it delivers on what it focuses on. For a solo creator who sells digital products and wants email automation — specifically the post-purchase sequence, buyer tagging, and ongoing broadcast communication — the Creator plan gives you everything you need in one place. The visual automation builder is genuinely usable without being a technical project, and the tag-based subscriber model stays manageable as your product catalog grows.

The case for Kit is strongest if you're already past the audience-building stage and want your follow-up email pipeline to run without manual work after each sale. It's also a reasonable choice if you want to consolidate landing pages, forms, and email under one tool rather than stitching together several.

Who should look elsewhere: if you're early-stage and your primary need right now is growing a newsletter audience with minimal cost, the free plan works — but be clear that you're deferring automation, not getting it free. If your list is already large or growing very fast, model out the per-subscriber costs carefully before committing; the pricing curve matters at scale. And if you need a sophisticated checkout experience, an affiliate program, or detailed funnel analytics, you'll still need to bolt those in from outside — Kit won't replace a dedicated sales platform.

For the specific job we describe in this hub — running an automated email sequence that starts the moment someone buys a digital product — Kit does that job cleanly and without unnecessary complexity. That's worth something for a solo operator who doesn't want to spend time maintaining infrastructure.
