Cloudways vs. Kinsta: Managed Hosting for AI-Built Sites, Compared

When you build sites with AI-assisted workflows, the last thing you want to manage is a server. Both Cloudways and Kinsta remove that burden, but they do it in fundamentally different ways and at meaningfully different price points. Cloudways is a management layer that sits on top of five major cloud providers — you pick the provider, pick the server size, and pay hourly for what you run. Kinsta is a finished, all-inclusive WordPress service on Google Cloud, priced by plan tiers based on visits and storage rather than raw infrastructure.

The right choice is not about which platform is objectively better. It is about which trade-offs match what you are actually building. If you are running a mix of small WordPress sites or PHP projects and want the lowest workable starting cost, those are different requirements than running a single high-revenue WooCommerce store where you want migrations, support and CDN bundled into one predictable monthly line item. This comparison lays out exactly what each service does and does not handle, using only the verified facts we have on hand.

The Core Difference

Cloudways is best understood as managed control. You still choose your underlying cloud provider — DigitalOcean, Vultr, Linode, AWS or Google Cloud — and you choose the server size that fits your budget and traffic. What Cloudways removes is the need to touch the command line: server provisioning, security patching, caching configuration and backups are handled through their panel. The billing is hourly and follows the server you pick, so a small server stays inexpensive and you can scale the server up from the panel when you need to, then scale it back down. There are no visit caps or bandwidth overages; you pay for the infrastructure you are running.

Kinsta is best understood as a finished service. You buy a plan, and that plan comes with a fixed number of WordPress installs, a visit allowance, storage, daily backups, CDN, and 24/7 support from WordPress engineers. The infrastructure is Google Cloud with isolated containers per site, and Kinsta manages all of it — including free unlimited migrations done by their team when you sign up. You do not resize a server or choose a provider; Kinsta handles placement across 30 data center locations. What you give up is flexibility: Kinsta only runs WordPress, and each plan tier is sized for a defined workload.

The practical difference shows up immediately in the billing model. Cloudways lets you host several different kinds of sites and apps on a single server and splits no costs between them beyond the base server price. Kinsta prices per set of installs and per site for heavier add-ons, so the cost scales with the number of WordPress properties you run rather than with raw server size. Neither model is wrong; they are optimised for different kinds of operators.

Feature Comparison

What You Can Host

Cloudways: WordPress and other PHP applications on one server — multiple sites and app types can share the same server. Kinsta: WordPress only, with each plan covering a defined number of installs; no support for non-WordPress PHP apps.

Infrastructure

Cloudways: Your choice of DigitalOcean, Vultr, Linode, AWS or Google Cloud; you select the provider and server tier at setup. Kinsta: Google Cloud only, with isolated containers per site and 30 data center locations available.

Staging and Workflow

Cloudways: Staging environments included; vertical server scaling from the panel lets you resize the server for launches without migrating. Kinsta: One-click staging included on all plans, plus a built-in application performance monitoring (APM) tool for diagnosing slowdowns.

Migrations and Support

Cloudways: Managed security patching and panel-based support; migrations are not done by their team as part of the standard service. Kinsta: Unlimited free migrations performed by their team for new customers, and 24/7 live chat staffed by WordPress engineers.

Backups and Extras

Cloudways: Off-site backups billed at $0.033 per GB; Cloudflare Enterprise CDN available as an add-on at $4.99 per month per domain. Kinsta: Daily backups included with 14-day retention on entry plans and CDN included in all plans; premium add-ons such as Redis cost $100 per month per site.

Scaling Behaviour

Cloudways: Resize the server up or down from the panel at any time; no visit caps or bandwidth overage charges applied by Cloudways itself. Kinsta: Traffic beyond the plan's visit or bandwidth allowance is billed as overages at $0.50 per 1,000 visits or $0.50 per GB of server bandwidth.

Pricing, Compared

Cloudways uses pay-as-you-go hourly billing with no visit caps applied at the platform level. On DigitalOcean, the Standard tier starts at $11 per month for 1 GB RAM, with the Premium tier at $14 per month for the same RAM level. Moving up, 2 GB RAM is $24 Standard or $28 Premium; 4 GB is $46 or $54; 8 GB is $88 or $99. Vultr and Linode start at $14 per month. AWS entry pricing starts at $20.56 per month and Google Cloud starts at $37.33 per month on Cloudways. Backups and the CDN add-on are billed separately on top of those base figures. Cloudways offers a 3-day free trial with no credit card required.

Kinsta prices by plan tier. Single-site plans start at $35 per month (or $350 per year, which equals roughly two months free) and cover 35,000 monthly visits, 20 GB of server bandwidth and 10 GB of storage. The tier Kinsta recommends for ecommerce and membership sites starts at $50 per month. Multi-site plans range from WP 2 at $70 per month up to WP 40 at $450 per month. Annual billing locks in pricing without renewal jumps. Overages apply when a site exceeds the plan's visit or bandwidth limits. First-time customers are covered by a 30-day money-back guarantee.

The honest summary is this: Cloudways is cheaper at the small end of the market and its bill follows the server size you have chosen rather than a visit ceiling. A small DigitalOcean Standard server at $11 per month can run multiple sites for less than Kinsta's entry plan. Kinsta costs more from the first site but bundles support, free migrations, CDN and daily backups into a single plan price — costs that add up separately on Cloudways.

Pros and Cons

Cloudways

  • Pro: Lowest starting cost of the two — $11 per month on DigitalOcean Standard, with no visit caps or bandwidth overages from Cloudways itself.
  • Pro: Flexible enough to host WordPress alongside other PHP apps on a single server, useful for teams building varied automation projects.
  • Pro: Panel-based vertical scaling lets you resize a server for a launch and resize it back down without migrating the site.
  • Con: Backups and CDN are paid add-ons rather than bundled; the true monthly cost depends on how many extras you need.
  • Con: Migrations are not performed by Cloudways as part of the standard service — you handle those yourself or arrange third-party help.
  • Con: Support is panel-based rather than staffed by WordPress specialists, which matters when a WordPress-specific issue needs diagnosis.

Kinsta

  • Pro: Unlimited free migrations done by Kinsta's team make moving existing WordPress sites straightforward without touching server configuration yourself.
  • Pro: CDN, daily backups with 14-day retention and 24/7 WordPress engineer support are included in every plan — no separate line items for core features.
  • Pro: Isolated containers per site on Google Cloud and a built-in APM tool give WordPress sites a contained, diagnosable environment.
  • Con: WordPress only — not an option if your automation workflow produces PHP apps or non-WordPress sites that need hosting on the same account.
  • Con: Traffic overages at $0.50 per 1,000 visits or $0.50 per GB can make the bill unpredictable when a site spikes beyond the plan's visit allowance.
  • Con: Premium add-ons such as Redis cost $100 per month per site, which adds significantly to the total cost for high-performance configurations.

Verdict: Which One Fits Your Site?

Choose Cloudways if you run several small sites, mix WordPress with other PHP projects, or want to keep starting costs as low as possible without a visit ceiling. The pay-as-you-go model and panel-based server scaling also make it a practical choice when you need to handle traffic spikes — resize up for a launch, resize back down afterward — without locking yourself into a plan tier that assumes peak load every month. Teams building and deploying varied AI-assisted projects across multiple domains will find the single-server, multi-site model efficient at this scale.

Choose Kinsta if one WordPress site — or a small set of them — carries real revenue: a WooCommerce store, an online course, a membership site. At that level, the bundled migrations, the around-the-clock WordPress engineer support, and the included CDN and backups are worth paying a premium for. The fixed plan pricing also makes budgeting straightforward as long as your traffic stays within the plan's visit allowance, and the 30-day money-back guarantee reduces the risk of trying it on a live site.

One limitation applies to both: neither Cloudways nor Kinsta includes email hosting. Plan a separate transactional and business email service regardless of which hosting platform you choose. That is an operational detail that often gets missed until something needs to send mail.
